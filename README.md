# Metabolic niche breadth determines protective competition among *Clostridioides difficile* strains
## Overview
Virulent strains of *Clostridioides difficile* infection cause severe diarhhea, colitis and even death, but avirulent strains can protect patients against by blocking virulent strain colonization. Pre-colonization by an avirulent *C. difficile* could become a live biotherapeutic approach against C. difficile disease if we could select strains for both low virulence and high protection. Here, we compared 21 clinical MLST1 isolates with ANI >99.84% spanning from high virulent to low virulent strains, and we investigated their ability to protect against severe disease caused by the hypervirulent VPI10463 strain. In a co-colonization mouse model, we saw that while some avirulent strains inhibited hypervirulence, others did not. We ruled out that the protective phenotype was driven by adaptive immunity, differences in the rest of the microbiota, and strain genetic determinants, including core-genome variation and accessory-gene content. Then, we searched for differences in their metabolic utilization profiles using BIOLOG phenotype microarray to assess 2,090 strain-by-metabolite conditions. Avirulent protection was correlated with the ability to utilize a broader set of carbon sources, a feature we term resource-use breadth. A consumer-resource model constrained by experimental data explained how an avirulent strain with a broader metabolic niche could exclude a narrower virulent strain, whereas narrower avirulent strains coexisted and failed to protect. These predictions were consistent with the *in vivo* observations. Comparing metabolic niche breadth to determine inter-strain competition outcomes offers an ecological rationale for selecting strains for live biotherapeutics..
