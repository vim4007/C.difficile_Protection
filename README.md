# Metabolic breadth of avirulent colonizers underlying protection against Clostridioides difficile infection
## Overview
Clostridioides difficile infection affects nearly 500,000 patients annually in the US, with recurrence in ~30% of cases despite antibiotic treatment. Colonization by an avirulent strain is known to protect against infection by a secondary virulent strain, but the mechanisms underlying this protection remain poorly understood. Here, we compared 21 clinical MLST1 isolates that have ANI >99.84% yet span the full range of virulence, and investigated their interaction with the hypervirulent VPI10463 strain in a mouse co-colonization model. We observed that while some avirulent strains confer protection against the hypervirulent strain, others do not, and the protection phenotype is independent of adaptive immunity, microbiome composition, and common genetic determinants, including core genome variation, and accessory gene content. Then, using BIOLOG phenotype microarray profiling across 2,090 strain-by-metabolite conditions, we discover that the avirulent protection is correlated with the ability to utilize a broader set of carbon sources, a feature we term metabolic breadth. Consistent with the in vivo observations, a consumer-resource model parameterized from these data predicts that an avirulent strain with wider metabolic breadth can exclude a virulent strain of narrower metabolic breadth, whereas avirulent strains with narrower metabolic breadth would coexist and fail to protect. These findings establish metabolic breadth as a determinant of inter-strain competition outcomes, offering an ecological framework for the rational selection of live biotherapeutics against C. difficile infection.
